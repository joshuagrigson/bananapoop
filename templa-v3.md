# Templa v3: scan it once, make anything that fits it

Sep 25, 2026 · third pass on Round 3 (@Steve). Builds on `round-3-templa-improved.md`.

## Bottom line

**Start with boats, not kitchens. Sell the scans as a pattern library, not only as a measuring tool.**

Two research passes today changed the map:

1. **Countertops just got a competitor.** Novaclad launched a free homeowner iPhone LiDAR app on **Sept 23, 2026**. It measures a kitchen in about five minutes and quotes countertops at the local shop's prices, with one fabricator per market. It started in St. Louis, and its founders own a granite shop. That proves the idea, and it also means countertops are no longer open ground.
2. **Two "obvious" verticals are already closed.** Pool safety covers have Latham MeasureGO (iPhone LiDAR, free) plus drone systems from Meyco and Merlin. Curved stairlifts have Lehner StairScan Pro (iPhone LiDAR to DXF), Handicare PhotoSurvey and Stannah's iPad app. Skip both.
3. **The widest-open gap is marine, and it is Steve's world.**
   - SeaDek owners buy a $29-56 Mylar kit, trace their boat by hand, mail it in, then do dry-fit revision rounds. That takes **13-19 business days**.
   - The pro alternative is a fabricator with a **$28-33K Prodim Proliner arm**, charging **about $600 just to scan**.
   - Canvas enclosures ($2.4-4.5K), cushions and windshields are still templated on the boat, by mail, or with the old part.
   - There are 11.96M registered US boats.
   - The tolerance is about 1/8 inch, which is easier than the 1/16 inch stone and glass need.

**The big idea: every boat you scan becomes a pattern that sells again.** XPEL's paint-protection-film business runs on a library of more than 80,000 vehicle patterns that shops rent for $199-299/month. Boats have no such library. After Templa scans one 2019 Sea Ray SPX 190, every other owner of that model can order pre-cut decking, cushions or a canvas enclosure **without anyone scanning or tracing anything**. Each scan makes the next sale free. That is a data moat no hardware company or Novaclad is building.

**It also joins Steve's two finalists.** The same boat scan is the 3D condition record DealDock needed for sight-unseen sales. Templa Marine and DealDock stop being competing bets and become one company with two revenue lines.

## 2. The company: four products on one engine

### A. Templa Marine (lead product, day 1)
- **Sold to:** the ~300 Marine Fabricators Association shops, SeaDek / Hydro-Turf certified fabricators, and canvas and upholstery shops.
- **What it does:** a fabricator (or the owner, through an App Clip link) walks the boat with an iPhone. Templa returns a DXF for foam decking, canvas patterns, cushion shapes and windshield outlines, with an accuracy score on every edge.
- **Price:**
  - $249/month per shop, unlimited scans, **or**
  - $59 per scan.
  - Either way the shop still charges its customers the $600 scan fee it charges today, so Templa pays for itself on the first job.
- **Why they buy:** no $30K Proliner, no second trip to the marina, and any worker can scan instead of the one trained templater.

### B. The Boat Pattern Library (the moat, from month 4)
- Every scan is tagged by make, model and year. Once a model has been scanned and verified by a real install, its pattern goes into the library.
- **Fabricators rent the library** (XPEL DAP model): $99-199/month, or $25-49 per pattern download.
- **Owners buy direct:** a "Fits your boat" link lets an owner pick their model and order pre-cut decking or cushions through a partner fabricator, with no scan at all. Templa takes 10-15%.
- The popular models (center consoles, pontoons, bass boats, wake boats) get covered first, which is where most boats are.

### C. DealDock, rebuilt on the scan (month 6+)
- A seller scans their boat once. The listing link includes a 3D walk-around, measured dimensions and a condition record. That is the "guided video inspection" from Round 3, but stronger, because it is a measured model instead of a video.
- Same money as Round 3: $149 buyer / $99 seller plus referral fees on escrow, loans, transport and insurance.
- **Cross-sell:** the buyer who just bought the boat is the most likely person on earth to order new decking or a new canvas enclosure, and their boat is already scanned.

### D. Home Fit (licensing and later verticals)
- **Shower glass:** partner with Smart Glazier. It already has glass shops on a phone app with typed-in measure sheets; Templa fills those sheets automatically. Needs the 1/16" test.
- **Hot tub covers:** sell a "fit guarantee" API to cover makers (The Cover Guy, MySpaCover and others). The customer scans the spa in 60 seconds through a link, and the maker stops eating remakes, or stops making customers eat them. Price: $5-15 per scan. It is low ticket, but this is the highest-volume direct-to-consumer measurement pain found.
- **Countertops:** don't fight Novaclad for fabricators. Go around it. Sell **brand-funded AR**: Roomvo shows that manufacturers pay so dealers get a visualizer free (7,000+ dealers). No stone brand (Cambria, MSI, Caesarstone) shows a real slab in the customer's **measured** room today; they use uploaded photos. Pitch a brand: "your slab in their real kitchen, with a quote, before a fabricator even visits." The brand pays, and fabricators get it free.

## 3. Josh's engine: the scan is the lead

Lead marketplaces sell junk:
- HomeAdvisor paid up to $7.2M in a 2023 FTC settlement over lead quality.
- Shared remodel leads cost $50-150 and close 6-15% of the time, so each job won costs $400-2,500.
- Exclusive leads run $50-200. Booked appointments set by a call center run **$200-400**.

A Templa lead is different: the customer has **already scanned the space and picked a product in AR**. Josh's team calls every scan within five minutes and books the appointment. That turns the booked-appointment price ($200-400) into a Templa product:

- **Marine:** "Get a quote for new decking" link on boat forums, marina partners and DealDock → owner scans → Josh's desk books the fabricator → the fabricator pays $150-250 per booked job, or Templa takes a cut of the order.
- **Glass and countertops later:** the same desk, the same scripts, a different vertical.

This is the part Novaclad's founders (fabricators) are not set up to run, and exactly the part Josh already runs.

**Day-60 decision:**

- Marine passes at 1/8": go full Templa Marine plus the pattern library.
- Marine passes and a 1/16" glass test passes: add glass in months 4-6.
- Marine fails at 1/8": the engine isn't ready for fabrication. Pivot to Quote/AR only (Hover-style estimating), or back to DealDock with scan-based listings.

## 6. Risks and the strongest case against

- **Marine is smaller than kitchens.** About 300 MFA shops and a SeaDek company around $32M in revenue (unverified) is a small base. Counter: marine is the *beachhead*, not the ceiling. The pattern library compounds, DealDock adds a transaction line, and the engine carries into glass, spas and brand AR. But if the question is "biggest market on day 1," countertops wins, and Novaclad is already there.
- **Seasonality.** Boat work peaks in spring. Build and sign shops in fall and winter, which is right now.
- **SeaDek could build its own app.** It already runs the Mylar process and certifies fabricators. Being the scanner SeaDek adopts or buys is a good outcome, so approach it as a partner early, not as a target.
- **Library accuracy between boats.** Hulls of the same model vary, and owners add aftermarket gear. Mark library patterns "verified on N installs." Always offer a 60-second scan check before cutting.
- **Novaclad might expand to marine.** It says "other fabricated finishes" are coming. Speed matters, and marine is not on its announced path.
- **Accuracy is still told, not observed.** "Extremely accurate" becomes a pitch only once the Proliner side-by-side is on paper.
