Discharge day · in the hospital room

Before you leave the hospital

Discharge happens fast, usually when you're both tired. Most of what goes wrong in the first week traces back to something that wasn't asked here. Check these off before the wheelchair arrives.

Papers in hand

  • Discharge summary, printedDiagnosis, what was done, what's next, in writing.
  • The new medication list, printedCopy it onto page 3 with the nurse while they're in the room.
  • Written care instructionsWound care, diet, activity limits, bathing, lifting.
  • Follow-up appointments, with datesIf they say "call to schedule," ask them to book it now.
  • Prescriptions sent to the pharmacy, confirmedAsk which ones are new and which replace something.
  • The after-hours number for the team that treated them

Questions for the discharge nurse

  • What should I watch for, and what exactly do I do if I see it?
  • Which symptoms mean call you, and which mean call 911?
  • What was stopped, changed, or added on the medication list, and why?
  • Can they shower? Climb stairs? Drive? Be alone? For how long?
  • What equipment do they need at home, and who is delivering it?
  • Is home health, physical therapy, or a visiting nurse ordered? Who calls whom?
  • What's the diet, in plain words?
  • When is the next appointment, and what should we bring?

Before you drive away

  • Pharmacy has the prescriptions and they're ready, or you know when
  • Equipment (walker, oxygen, commode, bed) is home or arriving today
  • Someone is staying the first night
  • Fridge has food that matches the diet
  • Pathway from car to bed is clear
  • Page 4 of this kit is filled in from the discharge papers

This kit organizes the instructions your care team gives you. It does not replace them. When this kit and your discharge papers disagree, the discharge papers win, and the after-hours number is the tiebreaker.

When to call, when to go

Your discharge papers list the warning signs for this condition. Copy them into the boxes below so nobody has to find the paperwork at 2 a.m. The general signs printed here apply to almost every discharge.

Call 911 now

Chest pain or pressure · trouble breathing · face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech · sudden severe confusion or can't wake them · heavy bleeding that won't stop · a fall with a head strike, or they can't get up · fever with shaking chills after surgery

Condition-specific, from the discharge papers:

Call the care team today (use the after-hours number if needed)

Fever · redness, swelling, warmth, or drainage at an incision or wound · new or worse pain not controlled by what was prescribed · not keeping food or fluids down · no urine for many hours, or burning · new swelling in a leg · new confusion, dizziness, or a near-fall · a missed or doubled medication you're unsure about

Condition-specific, from the discharge papers:

Watch and note (bring to the follow-up)

Appetite, sleep, energy, mood, bathroom changes, mild soreness that's improving. Write it on the daily log; it's what the doctor will ask about.

The week

The 7-day plan

A default rhythm for the week, to adjust to your discharge instructions. Check boxes as you go. The daily log on page 7 holds the numbers.

Day 1Home day
  • Medication changes sheet done, old bottles out
  • Pill organizer filled for the week
  • "When to call" page filled and posted
  • Fluids and a real meal, per the diet
  • Someone stays overnight
  • Confirm pharmacy pickup and equipment
Day 2Settle
  • First full day on the new medication schedule
  • Call to confirm every follow-up on page 6
  • Wound / incision check per instructions
  • Short walk or exercises, if allowed
  • Daily log started: vitals, pain, appetite
  • Help roster filled for the week
Day 3Watch
  • Compare today's log to yesterday's; note anything worse
  • Bathroom and fluids on track?
  • Pain controlled with what was prescribed?
  • Home health / nurse visit, if ordered
  • Refill anything running low
Day 4Midweek
  • Caregiver check: did you sleep? Who relieves you?
  • Activity up a notch only if the instructions allow
  • Write questions for the follow-up (page 10)
  • Fridge: still matches the diet?
Day 5Routine
  • Medications: any side effects to report?
  • Wound / incision check
  • Mood and sleep: note changes
  • Call the office with yellow-list items before the weekend
Day 6Prepare
  • Gather for the follow-up: this kit, med list, daily log, discharge papers
  • Confirm ride and time
  • Pill organizer refilled for next week
Day 7Review
  • Read the week's log together: better, same, or worse?
  • Decide what help continues and what can stop
  • Follow-up visit (often within 7 to 14 days)
  • Update the fridge sheet and medication list

Before they come home, or day 1

Setting up the house

Recovery happens in about four places: bed, bathroom, chair, kitchen. Make the path between them short, lit, and clear, and put what they need within arm's reach of each.

Bed

  • On the main floor if stairs are limited
  • Height lets feet touch the floor
  • Lamp, phone, water, glasses within reach
  • Night light to the bathroom
  • Extra pillows for positioning, if instructed
  • Commode or urinal nearby if the bathroom is far

Bathroom

  • Grab bars or a sturdy hold at toilet and shower
  • Shower chair and handheld sprayer if bathing is allowed
  • Non-slip mat; rugs removed
  • Raised toilet seat if standing up is hard

Chair & living space

  • A firm chair with arms, not the low sofa
  • Walker or cane parked where they sit
  • Remote, phone, water, tissues, medications list at hand
  • Cords, rugs, pet beds out of the path
  • Chair to bathroom and chair to kitchen both clear

Kitchen

  • Meals for the week matching the diet, portioned
  • Fluids they'll actually drink
  • Everyday items at counter height
  • Pill organizer on the counter, filled

Equipment

  • Walker / cane adjusted to their height
  • Oxygen set up and explained, tubing routed safely
  • Hospital bed, if ordered, placed and tested
  • Thermometer, BP cuff, scale if the team asked for readings
  • Wound supplies in one bin

Paper

  • "When to call" page posted
  • Medication list on the fridge
  • Discharge papers in one folder
  • This kit on the counter

Day 4 to 6 · bring to the first visit

Questions for the follow-up visit

Check the ones that apply, add your own from the week's log, and write the answers during the visit. Bring this kit, the medication sheet, and the daily log.

Appointment with: ____________________   Date: ____________

About recovery

  • Is this where you'd expect them to be a week out?
  • Here's the daily log. Does anything in it concern you?
  • What should improve next, and by when?
  • What activity is allowed now: stairs, driving, lifting, bathing, being alone?
  • What would make you want to see them sooner?

About medications

  • Here's the changes sheet. Is this list right?
  • Which of the new medications stop, and when?
  • We noticed ______ since starting ______. Related?
  • Can the old prescriptions be cancelled at the pharmacy?

About services

  • Should home health or therapy continue, change, or stop?
  • Is any equipment no longer needed, or newly needed?
  • Who coordinates between you and the specialist?
  • Which release lets you talk with me directly?
  • When is the next visit, and with whom?
